> **Abstract**—Production freight logistics planning overwhelmingly relies on deterministic cost-minimization engines (such as Google OR-Tools CP-SAT) that systematically eliminate operational slack and route cargo through brittle bottleneck corridors. Consequently, minor stochastic disruptions cascade into severe delivery delays, contractual demurrage penalties, and regulatory violations. In this paper, we propose **AEGIS**, a resilience-aware logistics planning engine that models classical symbolic search under adversarial uncertainty. AEGIS integrates foundational search algorithms (BFS, DFS, Uniform-Cost Search, A* with geodetic Haversine heuristics) with a Bayesian risk inference layer and forward-chaining ontological compliance verification under Indian MoRTH / CMVR 1989 standards. To discover structural vulnerabilities without relying on human-specified uncertainty sets, AEGIS incorporates a dual-mode adversarial curriculum generator: exact Minimax search with Alpha-Beta pruning for bounded networks ($N \le 25$) and ACCEL-inspired evolutionary regret mutation for large-scale logistics networks. Furthermore, an incremental plan repair engine patches disrupted sub-graphs with sub-20 ms latency while preserving unaffected execution prefixes. Evaluated across 360 paired experimental instances on the Indian National Highway network and synthetic multi-scale topologies against recent 2020–2026 baselines, AEGIS achieves a 64.2% reduction in post-disruption oracle regret compared to Google OR-Tools CP-SAT at an intentional nominal cost premium of only 4.6%, mapping the empirical Cost-versus-Regret Pareto frontier and demonstrating that adversarial curriculum stress-testing discovers operational failure modes 4.1× faster than random or historical sampling.

> **Index Terms**—Autonomous logistics planning, classical planning under uncertainty, adversarial disruption curricula, multi-objective A* search, regret minimization, plan repair, combinatorial optimization, Indian freight compliance.

## I. PROBLEM SUMMARY

Logistics planning in production environments is overwhelmingly solved as deterministic cost minimisation. Given a spatial transport graph of distribution centres, freight corridors, shipment consignments, and fleet capacities, an industrial solver—predominantly Google OR-Tools [18] or commercial mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) engines—computes the assignment of shipments to routes that minimizes nominal operational expenditures. The generated plan is mathematically optimal with respect to the cost matrix supplied at solve time. However, that cost matrix is merely a static snapshot. Real-world physical distribution networks experience continuous stochastic disruptions: monsoonal flash floods submerge low-lying highway corridors, labour union strikes halt major transshipment hubs, electronic toll plazas experience gridlock, and geopolitical or infrastructural failures sever primary freight arteries. The plan that was cheapest under static assumptions is routinely the plan that fails most catastrophically once disruptions materialize.

This structural fragility arises because classical cost optimisation systematically eliminates operational slack. A cost-minimal solver concentrates cargo flow onto a small subset of the cheapest, fastest corridors, pushes fleet utilization to capacity boundaries, and funnels multiple independent consignments through shared geographical bottlenecks. Under these razor-thin margins, a single localized disruption triggers severe cascading failures: missed delivery deadlines, substantial demurrage penalties, and driver hour violations. Conversely, a resilience-weighted plan that proactively diversifies transit corridors or introduces strategic buffer margins incurs a minor nominal cost premium, yet drastically reduces the cost actually incurred when disruptions strike. Despite this reality, standard commercial pipelines contain no mechanism to stress-test candidate plans against adversarial scenarios before physical dispatch.

Operations research provides two formal frameworks to address uncertainty: robust optimisation [1], [2], [4] and stochastic vehicle routing [5], [6]. Both approaches depend critically on the human practitioner pre-defining an explicit uncertainty set (such as budgeted parameters or Wasserstein ambiguity balls [2]) or an assumed probability distribution. Consequently, plan robustness is strictly bounded by human foresight: disruptions lying outside the assumed set receive zero protection, while low-probability, high-consequence tail events are rarely sampled. Meanwhile, recent machine learning advances (2020–2026) have explored adversarial curriculum generation and Unsupervised Environment Design (UED), notably PAIRED [7] and ACCEL [8]. The core insight from UED is that maximizing student regret—rather than raw difficulty—produces an effective, informative curriculum that drives zero-shot generalization. However, UED has been confined entirely to gradient-trained reinforcement learning policies. A symbolic planning engine does not learn via backpropagation, but it can be systematically stress-tested against an adversarial curriculum to quantify its empirical resilience frontier.

A critical third gap sits outside both paradigms: freight logistics is bound by physical, regulatory, and vehicle-class constraints. Under Indian Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) and Central Motor Vehicles Rules (CMVR 1989) regulations [20], commercial freight is partitioned into strict categories: Light Commercial Vehicles (LCV $\le$ 7.5T), Intermediate (ICV $\le$ 12T), Medium (MCV $\le$ 16.2T), Heavy (HCV $\le$ 25T), Multi-Axle (HCV-Multi $\le$ 40.2T), and Over-Dimensional Cargo (MHCV/ODC). Furthermore, hazardous goods (HAZMAT) face legal prohibitions on selected urban bypasses and tunnels. A naive cost-optimizing reroute that violates gross vehicle weight ratings or legal access rules represents an illegal operation rather than an effective recovery [20], [22]. Compliance checking must therefore be embedded directly inside the planning and stress-testing loop.

### Project Objectives
1. **Develop AEGIS**, a resilience-aware logistics planning engine integrating classical symbolic search (BFS, DFS, Uniform-Cost Search, A* with great-circle geodetic heuristics, hill-climbing) with a Bayesian disruption inference layer and forward-chaining ontological compliance checking.
2. **Implement a dual-mode adversarial scenario generator** combining exact Minimax with Alpha-Beta pruning (for bounded sub-networks) and evolutionary regret-based scenario mutation (ACCEL-style for large networks) that probes planner vulnerabilities under a global disruption severity budget.
3. **Provide incremental plan repair** that surgically patches disrupted sub-graphs while preserving unaffected execution prefixes.
4. **Establish a rigorous, reproducible head-to-head empirical benchmark** against Google OR-Tools CP-SAT [18] on identical problem instances across Indian freight corridors and synthetic multi-scale topologies, plotting the empirical Cost-versus-Regret Pareto frontier.

### Dataset and Problem Domain
The domain models freight transport across India's National Highway network (covering key hubs: Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Pune) parameterized with empirical state-wise diesel taxation (IOCL rates), road roughness indices, and National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) toll structures [19]. To rigorously evaluate scalability, experiments span synthetic networks across three scale tiers: Small (10–25 depots), Medium (50–100 depots), and Large (200–400 depots), structured into Hub-and-Spoke, Regional Grid, and Random topologies.

### Expected Outcome
AEGIS will demonstrate a statistically significant 42%–68% reduction in post-disruption regret against an optimal oracle at an average nominal cost premium of only 3.8%–6.2% over Google OR-Tools, proving that adversarial curriculum stress-testing discovers operational vulnerabilities far more effectively than uniform random or historical sampling.

## III. OUTCOME OF THE REVIEW

### 1. Gap / Issue Identified and the Proposed Solution
The comprehensive review of the 2020–2026 academic and patent literature reveals three fundamental disconnects:
- **The Operations Research Disconnect:** Recent robust and distributionally robust optimization formulations [1]–[4], [6] optimize min-max cost or regret under budgeted or Wasserstein uncertainty sets. However, they require uncertainty sets to be hand-specified or calibrated from historical traces [2]. Column generation schemes [1], [3] implement adversarial subproblems welded to algebraic MILP duality. They cannot interact with symbolic planners governed by non-linear ontological rules, and they terminate at a single conservative solution rather than mapping the full Pareto trade-off spectrum.
- **The Machine Learning Disconnect:** Unsupervised Environment Design (PAIRED [7], ACCEL [8]) conclusively demonstrated that regret-maximizing adversaries generate far more informative training environments than pure minimax or uniform domain randomization. However, UED has been developed exclusively for neural RL policies [5], [14]. Symbolic, non-learning planning engines [10], [11] have never been subjected to regret-driven curricula to audit their resilience boundaries.
- **The Regulatory & Physical Disconnect:** The reviewed patent landscape [21]–[24] and empirical network studies [19], [20] confirm that industrial logistics is shifting toward predictive monitoring and digital twins. Yet, every deployed system is either reactive (replanning only after physical failure occurs) or diagnostic (scoring risk without generating hardened routes). Crucially, existing routing engines fail to enforce physical multi-axle freight restrictions (Indian MoRTH / CMVR 1989 standards [20]) and hazardous materials routing within the resilience score.

**The Proposed Solution (AEGIS):** AEGIS bridges these disparate domains into a unified, modular architecture. It wraps a symbolic planning core (BFS, DFS, UCS, A* with geodetic Haversine heuristics) inside an adversarial stress-testing harness. A Bayesian risk inference engine computes prior disruption distributions [16]; a forward-chaining ontology engine enforces Indian legal and axle compliance [20]; an adversarial curriculum generator (exact Minimax with Alpha-Beta pruning for small subgraphs; evolutionary ACCEL-style mutation for large graphs) searches for regret-maximizing disruption scenarios under a severity budget. Finally, AEGIS evaluates plans head-to-head against Google OR-Tools CP-SAT [18] on identical instances to produce an empirical Cost-versus-Regret Pareto frontier.

### 2. Novelty of the Proposal (What is New)
AEGIS claims three distinct methodological and architectural contributions:
1. **Solver-Agnostic Adversarial Scenario Generation for Symbolic Planners:** Unlike algebraic column generation [1], [3], which requires MILP duality, AEGIS treats the planner as a black-box oracle scored by realized disruption regret. This allows the adversary to stress-test arbitrary symbolic, ontological, and rule-based solvers that resist mathematical programming reformulations.
2. **Transfer of the Regret-Driven Curriculum Objective from RL to Classical Planning:** AEGIS is the first framework to import the regret-maximizing curriculum objective of PAIRED [7] and ACCEL [8] into classical AI planning under uncertainty, proving empirically that regret-guided mutation uncovers catastrophic logistics failure modes 4.1× faster than uniform random sampling at matched compute budgets.
3. **Compliance-In-The-Loop Resilience Metric:** AEGIS incorporates Indian MoRTH / CMVR 1989 truck classification rules (payload and gross vehicle weight limits across LCV, ICV, MCV, HCV, HCV-Multi, MHCV) and hazardous goods routing directly into its search and regret metrics [20]. A candidate reroute that is nominally low-cost but legally or physically infeasible is penalized as an unmitigated failure rather than a valid recovery.

*Explicit Non-Claims:* AEGIS does not claim novelty for min-max regret as a mathematical objective, alpha-beta pruning as an algorithm, or outperforming Google OR-Tools on static nominal cost. The novelty is strictly the architectural synthesis that exposes and hardens against logistics brittleness.

### 2. Module Description
The AEGIS framework comprises eleven tightly integrated modules:
- **M1: API & Orchestration Service (FastAPI):** Exposes RESTful endpoints for network graph ingestion (`/v1/graph`), shipment dispatch (`/v1/shipments`), multi-objective planning (`/v1/plan`), disruption injection (`/v1/disrupt`), and Pareto frontier retrieval (`/v1/pareto`). Integrates `fuel_service.py` to calculate real-world Indian freight operating economics (diesel pump prices across Indian states, road quality indices, toll multipliers).
- **M2: Directed Transport Graph Engine:** Represents multi-modal road networks using in-memory adjacency lists (and rustworkx graph primitives). Features dynamic feeder corridor injection: resolves user addresses via OpenStreetMap (OSM) Nominatim geocoding and stitches origin/destination coordinates into the backbone national highway network with distance-scaled synthetic feeder edges.
- **M3: Multi-Algorithm Search Core:** Implements classical search algorithms from scratch: Breadth-First Search (BFS), Depth-First Search (DFS), Uniform-Cost Search (UCS), A* with geodetic Haversine heuristics (scaled by minimum per-kilometer cost to guarantee admissibility), Greedy Best-First Search, and Hill-Climbing with restarts for stop-sequencing optimization.
- **M4: Resilience Planner & Plan Repair:** Computes candidate paths across a grid of risk weights $\lambda \in \{0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0\}$. When disruptions materialize, an incremental plan repair engine patches only the affected sub-graph while preserving unaffected execution prefixes.
- **M5: Bayesian Risk Inference Layer:** Formulates a Bayesian belief network over disruption triggers (monsoon flooding, labor strikes, infrastructural breakdown). Computes exact posterior marginals on edge failure probabilities via variable elimination on bounded networks, with independent-union prior aggregation for rapid online execution.
- **M6: Forward-Chaining Ontological Compliance Engine:** Evaluates physical and legal feasibility against Indian MoRTH / CMVR 1989 standards [20]. Enforces vehicle payload and Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW) ratings, and filters out routes violating hazardous goods (HAZMAT) movement restrictions.
- **M7: Adversarial Disruption Curriculum Generator:** Searches the parameter space of network disruptions (route ID, type, severity $s \in [0,1]$, duration) subject to a global severity budget. Operates in two modes: exact Minimax with Alpha-Beta pruning for networks $N \le 25$, and evolutionary scenario mutation (ACCEL-inspired crossover and mutation of high-regret disruption sets [8]) for $N > 25$.
- **M8: Meta-Controller & $\lambda$-Pareto Filter:** Manages search strategy selection, evaluates candidate plans across the risk grid, and filters out dominated candidates to construct the non-dominated Cost-versus-Regret Pareto frontier.
- **M9: Execution & Streaming Pipeline:** Offloads long-running adversarial stress-testing jobs and publishes real-time simulation, disruption injection, and replanning lifecycle events over WebSockets to the Next.js visualizer.
- **M10: Benchmark Harness:** Executes Google OR-Tools CP-SAT [18] on identical problem instances. Subjects both AEGIS and OR-Tools plans to identical injected disruption suites and records head-to-head metrics in a unified benchmark comparison matrix.
- **M11: Persistence & Analytics Layer:** Stores depot graphs, fleet configurations, shipment manifests, and detailed benchmark records to support statistically inspectable comparative analysis.

## V. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS / EXPERIMENTAL SET-UP

**Hardware & Software Stack:** The core planning engine is developed in Python 3.11/3.12, organized into a clean monorepo architecture (`core/aegis_core`, `services/api`, `frontend`, `sdk`). The backend leverages FastAPI for high-performance async REST endpoints, Pydantic v2 for strict domain validation, rustworkx for high-throughput graph operations, and Google OR-Tools v9.10 (CP-SAT solver [18]) as the external baseline. The frontend is implemented in Next.js 14 (TypeScript) with Leaflet / OpenStreetMap mapping, Recharts, and custom glassmorphic telemetry dashboards. All benchmarks were executed on an Apple Silicon M-series testbed (12-core CPU, 36 GB unified memory, macOS Sonoma) with fixed random seeds for strict reproducibility.

**Dataset & Problem Domain:** Experiments were conducted on two complementary graph suites:
1. *Real-World Indian National Freight Backbone:* 15 primary hub nodes (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Pune, Nagpur, Jaipur, Lucknow, Kochi, Guwahati, Chandigarh, Indore) interconnected by 42 bidirectional national highway corridors [19]. Edges are calibrated with actual NHAI road distances, state-wise diesel pump prices (₹91.50/L base IOCL rate with state VAT adjustments), toll tax rates (₹1.00/km for LCV up to ₹2.80/km for MHCV), and historical road roughness multipliers.
2. *Synthetic Multi-Scale Topologies:* To establish theoretical scaling boundaries, synthetic graphs were generated across three scale tiers: Small (10–25 depots), Medium (50–100 depots), and Large (200–400 depots), across three structural topologies: Hub-and-Spoke, Regional Grid, and Random Geometric Graphs (density parameter $\delta = 0.25$). Shipment manifests span diverse weight categories (1,000 kg to 35,000 kg) mapped to Indian MoRTH truck classes (LCV, ICV, MCV, HCV, HCV-Multi, MHCV [20]) with tight and loose delivery deadlines.

**Disruption Model:** A disruption scenario is formalized as a set of tuples: $S = \{(e, \text{type}, s, d)\}$, where $e$ is the impacted edge, $\text{type} \in \{\text{weather}, \text{strike}, \text{roadwork}, \text{congestion}\}$, severity $s \in [0, 1]$ scales edge traversal time by $(1 + s)$, and $d$ is duration in hours. An edge with severity $s = 1.0$ represents a total structural blockage. To prevent the adversary from trivially severing all outgoing edges from a depot, a global severity budget $B = \sum s \le 0.35 |E|$ is enforced, mirroring the bounded capacity constraints of real-world crises.

**Hyperparameters & Evaluation Protocol:** Risk sweep weights $\lambda \in \{0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0\}$. Minimax search depth $d = 3$. Evolutionary scenario generator: population size $N = 50$, generations $G = 100$, mutation rate $p_m = 0.15$, crossover rate $p_c = 0.70$. Thirty paired instances were evaluated per scale-topology cell (360 total test runs). Statistical significance was assessed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (two-tailed, $\alpha = 0.05$) with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples).

## VI. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

### 1. Evaluation Metrics
To rigorously quantify performance, five primary evaluation metrics are defined:
- **Nominal Cost Gap ($\Delta C$):** Percentage cost difference between AEGIS plan $P$ and the cost-optimal Google OR-Tools plan $P_{OR}$ under undisrupted conditions:  
  $$\Delta C = \frac{\text{Cost}(P) - \text{Cost}(P_{OR})}{\text{Cost}(P_{OR})} \times 100\%$$
- **Disruption Oracle Regret ($R$):** Operational cost incurred by plan $P$ under realized disruption scenario $S$ compared to a post-hoc optimal oracle $P^*_S$:  
  $$R(P, S) = \text{Cost}(P \mid S) - \text{Cost}(P^*_S \mid S)$$  
  A lower regret indicates superior resilience.
- **Compliance Violation Rate ($V$):** Percentage of routes generated that violate MoRTH truck payload limits or HAZMAT corridor prohibitions [20]:  
  $$V = \frac{N_{\text{invalid}}}{N_{\text{total}}} \times 100\%$$
- **Replanning Latency:** Wall-clock time required to generate a repaired plan upon disruption realization, measured across the 50th, 95th, and 99th percentiles (ms).
- **Adversarial Efficiency Index (AEI):** Average regret induced in candidate plans per unit of compute budget (iterations):  
  $$\text{AEI} = \frac{R_{\text{induced}}}{C_{\text{compute}}}$$

### 3. Comparative Analysis
- **AEGIS versus Google OR-Tools CP-SAT:** Across all 360 paired experimental instances, Google OR-Tools [18] consistently identified the nominal cost-optimal path when no disruptions were present. However, because OR-Tools concentrates flow onto minimum-cost edges without regard for structural bottleneck fragility, its solutions degraded severely under adversarial disruption, suffering an average oracle regret of 54.2%. In contrast, AEGIS with balanced risk weighting ($\lambda = 1.0$) diversified corridor exposure, accepting an intentional nominal cost premium of 4.6% (₹148,800 vs ₹142,300) to achieve an average oracle regret of only 19.4% — a 64.2% relative reduction in post-disruption regret. A two-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirms that AEGIS's regret reduction is statistically significant across all scales ($p < 0.001, z = -14.22$).
- **Adversarial Curriculum versus Random/Historical Baselines:** As depicted in Fig. 3, the ACCEL-inspired evolutionary adversary induced substantially higher planner regret (48.5%) than uniform random scenario sampling (26.4% [9]) and historical prior sampling (31.5% [16]) under identical computational budgets (100 iterations). Uniform sampling wasted 62% of its evaluation budget on non-critical peripheral edges that induced zero planner regret. In contrast, the evolutionary adversary rapidly discovered critical shared cut-sets and bridge corridors in the transport topology.

### 4. Interpretation of Results
**The Price of Resilience:** In logistics networks, resilience is fundamentally an insurance problem: accepting a minor, predictable nominal cost premium during normal operations purchases massive protection against catastrophic tail risk. Furthermore, the ablation results in Table II demonstrate that compliance checking is not an ancillary filter but an indispensable component of the objective function. When compliance checking was disabled, the optimizer selected unpaved rural bypasses and restricted municipal corridors that appeared nominally cheap but could not physically support HCV multi-axle freight (yielding a 17.4% physical violation rate [20]). Scoring these plans against a compliance-checked oracle exposed their true failure (74.2% regret). The experimental results conclusively confirm that all four primary objectives set out in Section I were achieved.

### 5. Comparison with Related Work
Our findings validate and extend several contemporary 2020–2026 works in the literature:
- *Xiao et al. (2023) [11] & Bogyrbayeva et al. (2022) [10]:* We confirm Xiao et al.'s finding that localized plan repair preserves schedule stability. Crucially, our study resolves the empirical question left open in classical planning literature: we quantify the precise crossover boundary at **~28% network disruption** (Fig. 4). Below 28%, incremental plan repair is up to 11× faster (12.8 ms vs 142.0 ms) and retains >80% route stability; above 28%, structural damage is so pervasive that scratch replanning becomes computationally and qualitatively preferable.
- *Dennis et al. (2020) [7] & Parker-Holder et al. (2022) [8]:* We confirm that the regret-maximizing curriculum objective transfers successfully from neural RL agents to symbolic classical planners. Evolutionary level mutation (ACCEL) scales seamlessly to spatial transport graphs without requiring gradient computations.
- *Zhou et al. (2024) [2] & Lu et al. (2021) [1]:* Whereas traditional robust optimization constructs a single conservative plan for a fixed $\Gamma$ or Wasserstein ambiguity radius, AEGIS generates the entire Cost-versus-Regret Pareto frontier (Fig. 2), giving decision-makers full transparency over the cost-resilience trade-off.

### 6. Limitations and Challenges
Three limitations and deployment challenges were identified during the study:
1. *Minimax Tractability:* Exact Minimax search with Alpha-Beta pruning explodes combinatorially beyond $N = 25$ depots (branching factor $b > 15$), necessitating the evolutionary metaheuristic fallback for regional and national scales.
2. *Subjectivity in Prior Probabilities:* Bayesian network prior distributions require empirical incident calibration; in data-scarce transport corridors, priors rely on subjective heuristics.
3. *Sensor & Communication Latency:* In real-world highway deployments, physical road disruption telemetry can experience reporting delays of 15–30 minutes, which may delay the triggering of incremental replanning.

## VII. CONCLUSION

Modern freight logistics relies predominantly on deterministic cost optimization solvers such as Google OR-Tools [18], producing brittle, slack-free routing schedules that cascade into catastrophic failure under real-world disruptions. In this work, we developed and evaluated **AEGIS**, a novel resilience-aware logistics planning engine that integrates classical symbolic search (BFS, DFS, UCS, A* with geodetic heuristics) with Bayesian disruption inference and forward-chaining ontological compliance under Indian MoRTH / CMVR 1989 standards [20]. AEGIS stress-tests candidate plans against an adversarial curriculum generator that formulates disruption scenarios using exact Minimax below a measured tractability boundary and evolutionary regret mutation (ACCEL-style [8]) above it.

Across 360 paired experimental instances spanning the Indian National Highway network and synthetic multi-scale topologies, AEGIS demonstrated a **64.2% reduction in post-disruption oracle regret** compared to Google OR-Tools CP-SAT, requiring an average nominal cost premium of only **4.6%**. Ablation studies proved that adversarial curriculum generation discovers vulnerability modes 4.1× faster than uniform random sampling, while incremental plan repair delivers sub-15 ms replanning latency and >82% route stability for disruption frequencies below 28%. By embedding regulatory and axle-weight compliance inside the resilience objective, AEGIS eliminates illegal or physically infeasible reroutes.

**Scope for Future Work:** Future research will explore training Graph Neural Network (GNN) heuristics (extending GOOSE [13] and RL4CO [14]) to accelerate node expansions on massive continental road graphs, integrating multi-agent decentralized dispatching for fleet-level coordination, and deploying continuous automated telemetry pipelines using IoT electronic tolling (FASTag) data for real-time Bayesian prior updates.
